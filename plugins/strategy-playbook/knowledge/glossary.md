# Glossary

**Strategy** - A set of choices that have tradeoffs.

**Roadmap** - A set of requirements: the work you plan to do. Not a strategy.

**Yearly strategic plan** - Usually a list of work items grouped by theme. Not a strategy by this definition either.

**North star (vision)** - An aspirational end state that may take a long time to reach. A litmus test: does this work get us closer, or farther?

**Key outcomes** - What must be true for the north star to be reached, written so delivery is obvious. Often reused as planning themes.

**Problem statement** - A short doc that names a problem with evidence, the value of solving it, why now, and dated next steps. Intent with a date at the top.

**Leverage** - The positive framing of "why now": what solving this unlocks.

**Opportunities** - The inverse framing of "problems," for cultures that respond better to positive framing.

**First principles (guiding principles)** - Durable, non-controversial beliefs the strategy rests on. Each stands on its own.

**Scope and non-goals** - Where the strategy plays and where it deliberately doesn't, to raise the odds of success.

**Strategy bet** - A choice written as "X versus Y," where Y is another reasonable choice, plus why X wins, the accepted drawback, and how you manage it.

**Detailed bet** - The paragraph under a bet: the choice, why it beats the alternative, the accepted drawback, and the mitigation.

**Below the line** - Bets not active now. Promoted as active bets settle. Keep five to ten active.

**Tiger team** - A small group of peers, often cross-functional, organized around a single goal. Also V-team, virtual team, pod. Not a working group.

**Staffing and execution model** - A doc that tells managers who is needed, at what allocation, for how long, what they give up, and tracks who has committed.

**Informed captain (driver)** - The person who leads the work and makes the call.

**The reader test** - Four questions any reader must answer: What's the problem? What's most important to solve? What are the choices? Is it achievable?

**Likeliest dissenter** - The person most likely to disagree. Show them your draft first.

**Ring of support** - The widening circle of people aligned with the strategy, from your team out to executives.

**Feedback contract** - Your published promise for how you handle feedback.

**Feedback log** - A linked doc where every piece of feedback gets "will address" or "won't address" with a reason.

**Ruthless dissenter** - Someone powerful or noisy who can't disagree and commit. Escalate if they put the project at risk.

**Meme-orable** - Short and provocative enough to become everyday language.

**Day in the life** - A north star written as a short story of a person's working day once the vision is real.
